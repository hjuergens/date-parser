# Shared test cases for the "schedule" grammar, implemented in every date-rule-* module.
#
#   <input> => <expected>
#
# <expected> is a comma-separated list of ISO dates, "none" for an empty list, or "error".
# Periods are applied left to right with java.time semantics: months (M, Q = 3M, Y = 12M)
# are added first and the day is clamped to the end of the month, then days (D, W = 7D).
# B counts business days of the market; without one, Monday to Friday (only Saturdays and Sundays
# are skipped). "- 1B" counts backwards, "+ 0B" leaves the date as it is.
# A series [start : step : end] yields start + k * step for k = 0, 1, ... while <= end (with a step of nB: start + k * n
# business days); without a step every day. "]" at the start or "[" at the end leaves that endpoint out (ISO 80000-2), so
# [a : b[ stops before b. A series is an item of a list, and the shifts after it apply to each element, duplicates kept:
# "[2026-01-01 : 3M : 2026-12-31] > EOQ > 3RD WED IN MONTH" is the IMM dates of 2026. IF also takes a set: IF {SAT, SUN}.
# Include and exclude: "A & B" adds the dates of B, "A \\ B" removes them, sorted and without duplicates; B is a date, a
# series or a set of dates {1 JAN, 6 JAN}. Left to right, each shift applies to everything on its left: in
# "[a : b] & c + 1D" every date of the union moves a day. Dates in year: "> {1 FEB, 1 MAY}" replaces a date with these
# annual dates in its year, so "[T : 1Y : T + 2Y] > {1 FEB, 1 MAY}" is 1 February and 1 May of three years.
# Picks: "5TH [SPOT : 1Q : ] > EOQ > 3RD WED IN MONTH" is the 5th date of the item strictly after T, in the order the item
# yields them (MaturityRule's next(5, ... every(quarter))); "2ND LAST" counts from the last one. A series with an empty
# end, [SPOT : 1Q : ], is open and needs a pick in front of the item (a pick looks at up to 10000 elements); LAST needs
# a closed series. The item of a pick starts with a series or an event, where several dates come from. The end of a
# series is one date, never a series, so after the last ":" a "]" closes an open series.
# "> IMM" moves to the next IMM date strictly after the date: the third Wednesday of
# March, June, September or December. Which date "next" counts from is written in front of it: today
# ("T > IMM"), tomorrow ("TOM > IMM"), spot ("SPOT EURSEK > IMM") or spot + 1B; "SPOT - 1D > IMM" is on or after spot.
# "> SPOT [PAIR]" moves to the FX spot date: + 1B for USD against CAD, TRY, PHP or RUB,
# otherwise + 2B (also without pair). So 2015-12-31 > SPOT is 2016-01-04, although Jan 1 is
# a holiday.
#
# A schedule is a rule: a function of the base date and the market, both supplied when it is evaluated.
# "T" is the base date. A market only says which days are business days (B, SPOT); without one, only
# Saturdays and Sundays are skipped. The test cases name base date and market in a prefix that is not part
# of the language:  [<base>] <input> => ...  or  [<base> <market>] <input> => ...
# Markets for the tests are declared as  market <name> = <schedule>: Saturdays and Sundays plus the dates the
# schedule yields when it is evaluated with the first of January of a year as base date (the year before and
# after are checked too, as an observed holiday can fall into the neighbouring year).
#
# Holiday rules: "4 JUL" (day of month), "3 MON JAN" (n-th weekday), "LAST MON MAY" and "EASTER" (Western) are
# dates in the year of the base date. An observance "{SAT - 1D, SUN + 1D}" shifts a date by the first item for
# its weekday; the items don't chain. "> FOLLOWING", "> PRECEDING" and "> MODFOLLOWING" (following unless that
# leaves the month, then preceding) move to a business day of the market.
#
# Rolling: "TOM" is T + 1B, "SPOT [PAIR]" is T > SPOT [PAIR]. "> NEXT x" and "> PREV x" move to the first x
# strictly after or before the date; x is a weekday, BD (business day), IMM or an annual date such as 4 JUL.
# An ordinal counts: "> NEXT 3RD FRI" is the third Friday, the anchor found three times, each time from the one before
# ("> NEXT 2ND IMM", "> PREV 2ND 4 JUL"); the suffix must fit the number (1ST, 2ND, 3RD, 4TH, 11TH, 21ST), and 0TH is an
# error. "NEXT 3 FRI JAN" stays the annual date, the third Friday of January. A set is the nearest of its anchors:
# "> NEXT {MON, FRI}" is the next Monday or Friday, "> NEXT {1 FEB, 1 MAY}" the next of the two dates.
# "> BOW", "> EOW", "> BOM", "> EOM", "> BOQ", "> EOQ", "> BOH", "> EOH", "> BOY" and "> EOY" move to the first or last day
# of the week (Monday to Sunday), month, quarter, half year or year. H is a half year, 6M.
# Selection in a period: "> 3RD WED IN MONTH" is the third Wednesday in the month of the date, counted from the day before
# the month starts; "> 2ND LAST BD IN MONTH" counts back from the day after it ends, "LAST" alone is "1ST LAST". The
# periods are WEEK, MONTH, QUARTER, HALFYEAR and YEAR; the targets are those of NEXT, an annual date starting with LAST only
# in braces ("> 1ST {LAST MON MAY} IN YEAR"). An error if the match falls outside the period.
#
# Lunar months: "1 SHAWWAL" (Hijri, Umm al-Qura), "10 TISHRI" (Hebrew; ADAR is Adar I in a leap year, ADARII
# Adar II) and "1 ZHENGYUE" (Chinese, without leap months) are the day of that month in the Gregorian year of the
# base date; an error if it falls there twice or not at all. Their names are month names: "3 MON SHAWWAL" is an error.
# Equinoxes, solstices and solar terms: "EQUINOX MAR", "SOLSTICE JUN", "EQUINOX SEP" and "SOLSTICE DEC" are the days on which
# the sun's apparent longitude reaches 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees, "SOLARTERM 1" to "SOLARTERM 24" those of the Chinese solar
# terms, counted from Lichun (315 degrees; SOLARTERM 5 is Qingming, 15 degrees), all in the Gregorian year of the base
# date. The day is the one in UTC, or in a fixed offset written after it ("EQUINOX MAR UTC+9" for Japan), as the rule
# doesn't depend on the market. Computed after Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (VSOP87 with the terms of appendix III).
# Conditional dates: "24 DEC IF MON TUE WED THU" is the date on these weekdays and no date otherwise; IF closes
# an item of a list.
# Named events: "@FOMC" is every date of the event FOMC in the year of the base date, the shifts applied to each
# ("@FOMC + 1B"); "> NEXT @FOMC" and "> PREV @FOMC" move to the next or previous one. Events for the tests are declared
# as  event <name> = <schedule>, evaluated like a market with the first of January of the year as base date and kept
# if they fall into that year. An event with several dates in that year is an error at the end of a range.
# US employment report (non-farm payrolls): a forecast, not a fact. BLS publishes its release dates about a year ahead,
# and only those count; a shutdown can still move them (October and November 2025). The rule is BLS's working
# convention: the third Friday after the week (Sunday to Saturday) that contains the 12th, "12 MAY - 1D > NEXT SAT + 20D",
# the day before if that is a federal holiday (market XFED), and for the December report the first Friday on or after
# January 4. It is often, but not always, the first Friday of the month. The cases are scheduled dates.
# Keywords are reserved: "EASTER" and six-letter month names such as "TISHRI" are never a currency pair.
# examples/catalog.txt holds more cases, generated from a holiday rule catalog (scripts/catalog_cases.py).
# examples/date-holidays.txt holds more, generated from the rules of date-holidays (scripts/date-holidays/).
